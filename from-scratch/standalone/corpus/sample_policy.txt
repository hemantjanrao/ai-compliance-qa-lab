Internal AI Use Policy
Example Corp — training corpus for the AI QA lab (not legal advice)

Article 1 Definitions
High-risk AI means a system that is used to evaluate people for hiring, firing,
credit, or insurance. A general-purpose chatbot used only for internal FAQ
is not high-risk under this policy. Providers must document the intended
purpose of every system before it is deployed to employees or customers.

Article 5 Prohibited practices
The company must not use AI for social scoring of employees. The company
must not deploy real-time remote biometric identification in offices except
where a written legal basis exists and a human reviewer is in the loop.
Prompt-injection experiments against production systems are prohibited
unless they run in the isolated QA environment described in Article 9.

Article 6 Classification rules
A system is classified as high-risk when it materially influences a decision
about a person's access to work, money, or essential services. If the system
only drafts text that a human must approve, it is not high-risk, but the
human review must be real — a rubber-stamp does not count. Classification
must be recorded in the system card before go-live.

Article 9 Risk management
Every high-risk system must have a written risk-management process. The
process is reviewed at least once per year, and after any serious incident.
The process must include: a golden evaluation set, an adversarial prompt
suite, a quality gate that can block a release, and an owner who can be
named in an audit. Residual risk after mitigations must be documented.

Article 10 Data and ingest
Training and retrieval corpora must come from an allowlisted folder. Files
from email attachments or personal drives must not be ingested. Each ingest
run must be idempotent: delete the old index, then write a new one, and
record the embedding model name. Mixing two embedding models in one index
is a defect.

Article 13 Transparency
Users must be told when they are interacting with an AI system. Answers
that use retrieved policy text must cite the article number. If the policy
does not contain the answer, the system must refuse rather than guess.
